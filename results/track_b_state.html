2 · The same grid, read as affirmative doctrine only — machine-classified, not human-verified

Identical cells, different field: counts_as_affirmative_doctrine is true only where the speech act is an assertion or a denial and the text's own voice endorses it. This is the sole tally that may be used for a doctrinal count, and it is the machine's reading, not a coder's.

3 · The interpretation dimensions get their own axes and their own denominator

Four questions, four groups of bars. The denominator on every bar is the 47,641 term-carrying records this run produced — not the 23,385 passages of the corpus and not the candidate counts of figure 1, so a percentage here is not comparable to a shade there. Each is decided per proposition before any term is read, which is why none of them can appear as a matched-term count.

4 · Conceptual presence and doctrinal endorsement are different questions

47,262 records carry a concept that is represented in the passage; 22,818 of them are also endorsed as doctrine. The gap is not noise to be discarded: a condition, a question or an opponent's report is evidence that a proposition is on the page, and reported speech preserves evidence of competing beliefs. v2 deleted 54 such matches outright, under a blocked_by_speech_act state that no longer exists; v3 withholds endorsement and keeps the record.

8 · The binding constraint is a second coder, not more data

No human annotation has been performed. The blinded packet now carries 6,199 propositions across 48 value strata, 65 works and 8 speech acts (11 rare strata taken as a census), with a published codebook, a separate answer key and seed 20261101. 4,699 of them are propositions the detector flagged, and 1,500 are a simple random draw from the 992,144 propositions it produced no record for — two disjoint strata, 900 cue-negative (π=0.000952549286) and 600 cue-positive (π=0.012682040118), each row weighted 1/π. They are there because a sample of hits contains no misses: the candidate rows can bound precision and cannot measure the detector's recall. It has never been coded, so this repository contains no κ, no precision and no recall — and the scorer refuses to run on one coder, on the same sheet supplied twice, or on a probe row with no weight. Nothing in the figures above is evidence that common ancestry is possible or impossible: all four competing models predict the same non-detection pattern in a one-translation English corpus. This instrument is multilingual-ready, not multilingual: every detector here is an English translation-text pattern.
