\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Recurring narratives across cultures raise the possibility of shared ancestry,
but similarity alone cannot distinguish common descent from borrowing or
independent invention. This paper presents a source-first framework for
investigating narrative relationships without presuming a universal origin, and
reports what three independent computational instruments --- a lexical
retriever, a structural comparison engine, and a divine-concept state coder ---
do and do not show when that framework is applied. Attested versions are
decomposed into source-bound events, participants, motifs and outcomes;
descriptive similarity is separated from linguistic and historical evidence; and
proposed relationships are tested against dependent witnesses, generic motifs,
alternative codings and competing models.

A cleaned 65-work English-translation corpus (23{,}385 eligible passages,
8.73 million words) was used as a retrieval instrument. Of 9{,}428 cross-work
candidate pairs, 8{,}677 (92.0\%) join two works already linked by a configured
source-dependence rule. A second engine compares coded narrative structure: 14
versions in four comparison classes, 94 events carrying 218 participant role
assertions, 76 causal links and 38 preregistered motifs, scored as five separate
components across 91 version pairs rather than a blended percentage. Known
dependent witnesses rank above every predicted-unrelated pair (minimum 0.632
against maximum 0.045), and re-coding every participant name leaves all 91 pair
scores unchanged. Two results cut against the expectations of this research
tradition: the sequence score is almost insensitive to event order (mean drop
0.0186 under permutation, below a preregistered 0.02 floor, for arithmetic
reasons identified rather than tuned away), and structural and lexical
similarity correlate only weakly and without established significance (Spearman
\(\rho = 0.264\); a quadratic-assignment permutation test on version labels
gives two-tailed \(p = 0.065\), so the textbook \(p = 0.019\) rested on an
independence the data do not have). The paper therefore abandons the composite
similarity score, emits no ancestry estimate, and treats independent invention
and unresolved relationships as substantive outcomes.

A third instrument tests the precondition of this project's own motive claim ---
that ``god'' names one concept worldwide, so that its recurrence would indicate
one invention. Its first version, a vocabulary census of ten analyst-authored
divine-concept grammars, reported that no grammar was attested in all 65 works
and read that as showing the motive claim has no stable referent. \emph{That
inference is withdrawn here and is not a result of this paper}: the census
measured one generation's English translation vocabulary, and non-detection in
such a corpus is predicted equally by every competing model of inheritance, so
it discriminates between none of them. The census's companion finding did not
survive an audit either (Section~\ref{sec:audit}). The rebuilt instrument codes
divine-concept \emph{states} instead of term vocabularies: twelve dimensions
declared analytically distinct rather than measured orthogonal, applied proposition by proposition,
emitting 47{,}641
candidate records over 36{,}775 propositions in 34{,}584 utterance windows, and
answering three questions per record separately --- whether the concept is
represented, whether the voice the text itself endorses affirms it, and whether
it was historically transmitted. \field{historical\_transmission} is
\texttt{not\_yet\_assessable} in all 47{,}641 records; nine dimension values are
published as undetectable by this instrument rather than absent; the passage-pair
candidate set is enumerated exactly by an inverted index---22{,}940{,}506 cross-work
pairs sharing a coded state, where the approximate blocking this layer first shipped
recovered a measured 2.35\% of them---and a missing edge is never read as a missing
relationship; and a blinded 6{,}199-proposition annotation packet ---
4{,}699 flagged candidates plus 1{,}500 weighted no-record probes, so that recall
as well as precision is eventually estimable --- is
ready for two independent coders, has never been opened, and therefore
corresponds to no reliability figure anywhere in this paper. The frame ships as
a methodology with four competing historical models, priced operator derivations
and licence conditions on reporting them; the model-comparison layer's own
output is \texttt{not\_yet\_testable}. What the instrument \emph{does} say, once
each dimension is measured against a chance line drawn from this corpus's own
value frequencies, is a set of findings about the instrument: three of its twelve
dimensions fire on no work at all, the speaker-attribution dimension clears its
own base rate by \(+0.006\), the exact-match rule the frame declares has no
defined answer on \(0.567\) of the single-valued cells it is applied to, and the
one prediction that separates M4 from a second null reverses on raw similarity
(\(-0.177\)) and ties once each dimension's base rate is subtracted
(\(-0.017\)) --- so the registered falsification hook is published as
scale-dependent instead of being read as a refutation of the model or of the
corpus. The further claim that no such being
exists remains outside the reach of any textual method, in either direction.
Global comparison is defensible as candidate discovery and falsification;
reconstruction must be restricted to elements supported by explicit historical
models, and 455 unit tests plus 7 parameterised subtests and the result
validators enforce those boundaries.

\section{Introduction}

Recurring stories and motifs are conspicuous across human cultures. Storm beings
confront serpentine adversaries; floods destroy or renew human communities;
primordial bodies become parts of the cosmos; heroes descend among the dead.
Such resemblances invite a historical question: did particular narratives
descend from shared ancestors, travel between communities, or arise
independently under similar constraints?

The question is legitimate, but its most ambitious answers are often
methodologically weak. A collection of similarities cannot distinguish
inheritance from borrowing, and neither can it distinguish either from
convergence. Broad labels such as ``flood myth'', ``dying god'' or
``dragon-slayer'' can conceal differences in agents, actions, sequence, function
and outcome. The problem is therefore not simply to discover parallels. It is to
determine which parallels require a historical explanation, which explanations
are compatible with the evidence, and where reconstruction must stop.

This paper proposes and applies a source-first comparative framework. Its
primary objects are attested narrative versions, not religions treated as
unified systems. It separates descriptive resemblance from historical
relationship, composition from attestation, and reconstruction from conjecture.
It also treats independent invention and unresolved relationship as substantive
outcomes rather than failed comparisons.

The analysis addresses four questions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
  \item Can narrative similarities be coded without erasing culturally specific
        meanings?
  \item Do shared structures provide evidence of common descent or transmission
        once generic motifs and dependent sources are controlled?
  \item Can competing historical models be tested against contradictory evidence
        and appropriate null cases?
  \item What elements, if any, can be reconstructed without inventing an
        ancestral story?
\end{enumerate}
For each specified element or event alignment, the study compares inheritance,
convergence, contact and mixed-history models using preregistered differential
predictions. Whether those elements form one or multiple families is assessed
separately. A universal-origin model is neither assumed nor tested by this pilot;
it would require its own scope, predictions and evidence.

\section{Concepts and competing explanations}

A \textbf{motif} is a recurring element, whereas a \textbf{narrative} is an
ordered and causally connected sequence involving participants and events. A
\textbf{tradition} is a historically situated body of transmitted material
associated with communities, languages and institutions. A \textbf{religion} may
include narratives but also practices, ethics, institutions and theological
claims. Similarity between narratives therefore does not establish equivalence
between religions.

A \textbf{narrative version} denotes the particular sequence attested in an
identified passage, performance, inscription or iconographic context. This
distinction matters because a figure may appear in several stories that cannot
legitimately be combined into one synthetic ``myth''. Likewise, a late record
directly attests the story at its recording date, but its earlier history
requires separate evidence.

Four process hypotheses are evaluated for each specified element or alignment:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{H1, common descent:} shared material was inherited from an
        ancestral narrative tradition.
  \item \textbf{H2, independent invention:} similar features emerged
        independently through recurring experiences, cognitive dispositions,
        ecological conditions or narrative constraints.
  \item \textbf{H3, transmission:} material was borrowed directly or indirectly
        between historically connected communities.
  \item \textbf{H4, mixed history:} inheritance, borrowing, innovation and loss
        operated together.
\end{itemize}
A separate structural question asks whether the versions form one or multiple
families. These possibilities may apply to different elements of the same
narrative. A borrowed episode can be integrated into an inherited cosmology; a
widespread motif can receive an independently invented sequence. Models that make
identical predictions for the surviving evidence remain unresolved.

\section{Materials and methods}

\subsection{Source and dating protocol}

Primary witnesses, critical editions, original-language texts and transparent
scholarly translations take priority. Peer-reviewed historical, linguistic,
archaeological and anthropological research supplies context and competing
interpretations. Modern retellings and general websites are discovery aids, not
evidence for historical claims.

Every material observation requires an exact passage, tablet, hymn, chapter,
line or section locator. Original wording and consequential translation choices
must be inspected where accessible. For oral and Indigenous material, recording
context, collector, community authorisation and restrictions form part of the
evidence.

Four dates are distinguished: composition of an expression, production of a
surviving witness, recording of a performance, and inferred age of the narrative.
These dates may differ substantially. A late manuscript can preserve an early
composition; a late ethnographic record can preserve older oral material without
thereby proving great antiquity. Section~\ref{sec:coder} adds the consequence
that the divinity instrument draws from that distinction: an \emph{earlier
surviving witness} is not necessarily an earlier composition, so dates enter the
design as ranges with a stated basis, not as points.

\subsection{Narrative decomposition}

Each version is represented at three levels:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Attested description:} source wording, participants, actions,
        order, context and outcome.
  \item \textbf{Comparative coding:} versioned motif, role, symbol and event
        categories with explicit definitions.
  \item \textbf{Historical interpretation:} proposed relationships,
        transformations and reconstructions linked to supporting and
        contradicting evidence.
\end{enumerate}
Source-native descriptions are retained alongside comparative codes. Thompson's
motif index and the ATU tale-type system aid discovery where applicable
\citep{thompson1955,uther2004}. Neither is an ontology of narrative structure: both index
and classify recurring elements, neither aligns the ordered event sequences this framework
compares, and this pilot therefore uses them for discovery only.
Character identity is separated from narrative role, so shared storm
associations do not make two figures identical. Alternative segmentations and
disputed codings remain available for sensitivity analysis.

\subsection{Similarity without ancestry by fiat}

Descriptive similarity is calculated independently of linguistic and transmission
evidence, and it is reported as separate components rather than blended. The
index originally specified for this framework was
\begin{equation}
  S_{\mathrm{desc}} = 100\bigl(0.40\,S_{\mathrm{structure}}
    + 0.25\,S_{\mathrm{motif}} + 0.20\,S_{\mathrm{role}}
    + 0.15\,S_{\mathrm{symbol}}\bigr),
  \label{eq:composite}
\end{equation}
where each component is normalised between zero and one. That composite is
\textbf{not used} in the implemented protocol (Section~\ref{sec:structural}).
Averaging components measured on different assessed domains would require a
missing component to be imputed or silently renormalised, and it would destroy
the pattern that matters: a pair can share most of its adjudicated motifs while
aligning almost no events, or the reverse. The component weights were
research-design choices subject to sensitivity analysis, not validated historical
constants, and that analysis confirms the ordering is bound to alignment
parameters rather than to the weights.

These scores answer only ``How similar are these coded versions?'' They do not
answer ``How probable is common ancestry?'' Linguistic relationship,
chronological feasibility, documented contact, verbal correspondence, shared
errors, distribution and contradictory evidence are assessed separately.
Numerical historical probabilities are admissible only under a calibrated model.

\subsection{Controls and falsification}

A global comparison is vulnerable to selection bias because investigators can
find some shared motif among almost any sufficiently large pair of corpora. The
framework therefore uses positive controls with independently supported
relationships, negative and matched controls, and narrative categories not used
to design the target comparison.

Null models must preserve motif prevalence, co-occurrence, genre, period, corpus
size, language-family clustering and dependent witnesses. Unconstrained random
pairings would exaggerate significance. Confirmatory tests are preregistered,
report effect sizes and uncertainty, and correct for multiple comparisons.
Results are retested under alternative translations, annotations, weights and
exclusion of late or dependent sources.

Phylogenetic studies of folktales show that narrative distributions can be analysed
with tree methods, and that the tree a study returns depends on which characters are
coded and which model is fitted \citep{tehrani2013,dasilva2016}. Their methods do not
license transferring their conclusions to unrelated narratives without new evidence.

A proposed historical relationship is weakened when its distinctive shared
sequence reduces to a generic motif, when its chronology is incompatible, when
apparent independent witnesses are textually dependent, or when a preregistered
contact or convergence model fits held-out evidence better. Repeated failure of
a single-origin model across independent panels would limit or reject that model
without pretending to prove that no remote historical connection ever existed.

\subsection{Reconstruction rule}

Support for a historical relationship does not by itself identify ancestral
features. Ancestral states require an explicit genealogy or network and a model
of transformation, loss and borrowing. Shared elements are not assembled into
fluent new prose. \textbf{Attested} describes evidence in surviving witnesses;
proposed ancestral elements are labelled \textbf{comparatively reconstructed},
\textbf{conjectural} or \textbf{unrecoverable}. Competing models and unexplained
elements are reported together.

\section{Pilot design}

The pilot uses three panels to prevent the analytical vocabulary from being
tailored to one desired relationship. Panel A examines six combat narratives or
proposed narrative groupings: Indra--Vritra, the Hittite Storm God--Illuyanka,
Zeus--Typhon, Marduk--Tiamat, Thor--Jormungand, and Perun--Veles. They are
candidates for comparison, not presumed descendants of one story. Panel B
compares selected flood, destruction and renewal narratives from Mesopotamian,
Hebrew and geographically distant traditions. Panel C compares creation through
bodily division in the Purusha, Ymir and Pangu materials.

The panels test different evidentiary problems. Panel A asks whether an apparent
``storm god versus serpent'' category survives event-level comparison. Panel B
separates regional transmission questions from the global distribution of flood
stories and from the problem of late recording. Panel C tests whether a broad
shared theme also contains a specific shared sequence and body-to-cosmos mapping.

No version is merged with another episode involving the same figure. In
particular, Thor's fishing encounter and his final conflict with Jormungand are
distinct attestations. Nor is a modern scholarly reconstruction treated as
equivalent to a surviving narrative: this distinction is essential for
Perun--Veles.

\section{Pilot analysis and computational results}

\subsection{Combat with a serpentine or primordial adversary}

The first panel does not support treating its six candidates as interchangeable
versions of one story. The most consequential distinction is between a shared
combat motif and a shared event sequence (Table~\ref{tab:combat}). The anchors and the
attested structures in that table are stated as the scholarly editions in which these
narratives are standardly read give them
\citep{jamison2014,hoffner1998,west1966,lambert2013,larrington2014,faulkes1987}; they are
the source of the description, and none of them is treated here as evidence for a
historical relationship. They are also not the texts this instrument read. Every machine
score reported below comes from the public-domain English translations listed in
Table~\ref{tab:editions}, and the difference is disclosed rather than left to the reader
because a citation is not a witness: an anchor in an edition nobody consulted here can
motivate a comparison, and cannot carry one. Two rows of Table~\ref{tab:combat}, the two
Illuyanka versions, and the \emph{Rigveda} 1.32 anchor name material that has no text in
this corpus at all, so they contribute no score --- and unlike the reconstruction case
below, they are quoted from a published translation of a surviving witness rather than
from a modern reconstruction, which is why they belong in a descriptive table that no
instrument feeds.

Perun--Veles is excluded from Table~\ref{tab:combat} because no directly
attested pagan narrative equivalent to these texts is supplied. Its proposed
combat belongs in a separate reconstruction case; using the reconstruction as
another independent witness would be circular.

One narrower candidate alignment for future investigation would include an
obstructing adversary, divine weapon, ordered combat and water release. This
pilot neither establishes that it is the strongest alignment nor demonstrates
that it was inherited. Removing or changing the last event changes the historical
question, but descriptive difference alone does not disprove common descent: a
model must specify which features it expects to remain conserved.

This does not eliminate historical relationships among the Eurasian materials.
Comparative Indo-European research supplies a disciplined precedent for investigating
inherited poetic and mythological material, and it is cited here for that method
\citep{watkins1995,west2007}. No reconstruction drawn from that literature is used as
evidence anywhere in this paper, because a reconstruction is not a surviving witness ---
the same ground on which Perun--Veles was excluded above.
Anatolian and Near Eastern contacts make borrowing and mixed histories serious
alternatives. A narrative family must be defined by shared innovations under an
explicit model, not by a modern category that combines different outcomes.

\subsection{Flood, destruction and renewal}

The Mesopotamian flood account in \emph{Gilgamesh} XI \citep{george2003} and Genesis 6--9 contain
much more specific correspondences than the bare occurrence of a catastrophic
flood: a chosen survivor, construction of a vessel, preservation of life,
grounding and bird dispatch form part of the comparison. Their historical
relationship must nevertheless be investigated through particular texts,
languages, chronologies and regional transmission, not through the general
category ``flood myth''.

A distant flood episode does not automatically add another independent descendant
of the same story. The flood in the \emph{Popol Vuh}, for example, belongs to a
sequence of unsuccessful creations and the destruction of the wooden people. Its
agents, victims, purpose and outcome differ from the survival narratives above.
The early-modern recording and transmission history of the \emph{Popol Vuh} also
form part of the evidence; its narrative cannot be assigned an unsupported
primordial date merely because it records Indigenous tradition.

The methodological result is that ``flood'' is too coarse to serve as the
principal historical unit. Ordered event combinations and their cultural
functions provide a stronger comparison, while ecological plausibility and later
religious contact remain live alternative explanations.

\subsection{Creation through bodily division}

\emph{Rigveda} 10.90 presents Purusha in a sacrificial account whose divisions
generate cosmic and social categories. In \emph{Gylfaginning}, Ymir is killed and
his flesh, blood, bones and skull furnish materials for the created world. The
Pangu tradition likewise associates a primordial body with cosmic transformation,
but its textual history and narrative sequence must be established from early
attestations rather than modern summaries.

The shared proposition ``the world derives from a primordial body'' is
significant as a recurring motif. It is not yet a shared ancestral narrative. A
defensible comparison must ask who acts, whether sacrifice or killing occurs,
which body parts map to which cosmic elements, in what order, and what social or
theological work the account performs. Different answers can reduce an apparently
exact parallel to a broad conceptual resemblance.

\subsection{Scope of the pilot results}

This illustrative source-critical pilot identifies descriptive heterogeneity in
selected cases and motivates event-level testing. It does not estimate how often
motif groupings fail, discriminate among historical models, or test universal
origin.

The same panels were later coded and scored, so the qualitative claims above
became measurements rather than assertions (Section~\ref{sec:structural}).
Indra--Vritra against Typhon returns \(S_{\mathrm{sequence}} = 0.221\), Thor's
fishing episode against Indra--Vritra 0.156, and Ymir against Pangu 0.154 ---
none of them the high figure a ``same story'' category would predict. The motif
component is thinner still and must be read with its domain: 0.412 for
Indra--Vritra--Typhon over two adjudicated motifs, 0.000 for Thor's fishing
against Indra--Vritra over two, and 0.250 for Ymir--Pangu over two. A score
computed over two motifs is arithmetic, not evidence, which is why the validator
publishes domain size and flags every such pair. The water-release and
corpse-division distinctions that Sections~5.1 and 5.3 raise by inspection are
therefore exactly what the instrument fails to find shared, and the agreement
between the reading and the measurement is a consistency check rather than an
independent confirmation: the same analyst produced both.

Accordingly, no global similarity percentage or ancestral story is reported.
Independent event-level annotation, control scoring and source-critical
investigation remain necessary before historical conclusions. A reconstruction
that cannot survive those steps should be abandoned, not made more imaginative.

\subsection{Computational baseline experiment}

A reproducible baseline was run on 42 English-language works and translations
comprising 7{,}549{,}234 words. Its own run summary records the imbalance that made it
a pipeline test rather than a global sample: 15 of those 42 works carry the South Asia
region label and 10 carry Europe, while no other label reaches 4.
Fixed 350-word passages were compared using
TF-IDF with unigrams and bigrams. The run retained 9{,}648 cross-work candidate
pairs; 18{,}963 random cross-work pairs had mean similarity 0.0141 and a 99th
percentile of 0.0512. All source hashes and 21{,}580 passage offsets were
validated, and repeated runs produced byte-identical result files.
Every figure in this paragraph is re-derived by \texttt{tests/test\_pipeline.py} from the
two artifacts of that run which still ship --- \texttt{results/run\_summary.json} and
\texttt{results/candidate\_pairs.jsonl}. What does not survive is the derived passage file
whose SHA-256 that summary records: the later 65-work rebuild wrote over it, so the
baseline is reported here as a historical result whose inputs are no longer in the tree,
not as a run a reader can repeat.

Known-overlap controls were recovered: a Dhammapada passage shared between two
Project Gutenberg sources scored 0.9119, while a Qur'an anthology overlap scored
0.6022. Manual inspection also identified direct Bible--Book of Mormon
dependence and repeated Mahabharata material. These results support use of the
method for detecting textual reuse.

The false matches are more important for model criticism. The strongest inspected
Hindu--Biblical pair, at \(0.5254\), sets a passage of 1 Samuel 8 against an
editorial note on page 174 of the \emph{Ramayana} volume, which quotes that chapter
at length and signs it \textit{I. Samuel, VIII}. The strongest Ramayana--Poetic Edda
pair, at \(0.2207\), places two index columns side by side. A Popol Vuh--secondary
synthesis pair at \(0.5483\) reproduces the Xibalba trick of the red-hot stone in
close wording and so cannot be treated as independent corroboration. Each of these
three is the maximum of its work-pair class in \texttt{results/candidate\_pairs.jsonl},
and \texttt{tests/test\_pipeline.py} recomputes all three maxima from that file rather
than trusting the score quoted here. Such cases demonstrate the need for
apparatus filtering and a source-dependence graph before comparative
interpretation.

The baseline also failed as a measure of narrative order. In the main run, original
passages compared with word-shuffled versions of themselves retained mean similarity
\(0.6740\) across the \(256\) sampled passages, which is what an order-blind bag of
unigrams and bigrams should do. The surviving artifact of that run records this one
figure and no n-gram split, so the split is taken from the current 65-work snapshot
instead, where all three configurations are published in
\texttt{results/v2\_run\_summary.json}: word-shuffled self-comparison retains
\(0.7125\), sentence-shuffled retains \(0.9858\), and bigrams alone fall to \(0.1071\)
under word shuffling. Bigrams are sensitive to local wording, and even they do not
encode event structure. Consequently, the experiment validates provenance and
candidate retrieval, not historical or structural inference.

\subsection{Relevance formulation and graph semantics}

The candidate-retrieval score is explicit. For term \(t\) in passage \(d\), the
implementation uses sublinear term frequency and smoothed inverse document
frequency,
\begin{equation}
  tf(t,d) = 1 + \ln \mathrm{count}(t,d), \qquad
  \mathrm{idf}(t) = \ln \frac{1+N}{1+\mathrm{df}(t)} + 1 .
\end{equation}
After English stop-word removal, unigram and bigram extraction, feature capping
and \(L_2\) normalisation, lexical relevance is the cosine
\begin{equation}
  R_{\mathrm{lex}}(p,q) = \frac{v_p \cdot v_q}{\lVert v_p\rVert\,\lVert v_q\rVert}.
\end{equation}
A passage pair enters the candidate set at \(R_{\mathrm{lex}} \ge 0.15\), with at
most six cross-work neighbours retained per passage. For a work-level review
ranking, the strongest eligible passage pair is used:
\begin{equation}
  R_{\mathrm{work}}(A,B) = \max_{p \in A,\, q \in B} R_{\mathrm{lex}}(p,q).
\end{equation}
This maximum is a triage heuristic, not a work-level historical score. Longer
works provide more opportunities for a high maximum, and repeated candidates
within one work pair are not independent observations. Random and length-matched
controls estimate how unusual scores are under limited sampling models; they do
not estimate the probability of transmission or ancestry.

The relevance graph is therefore read as \field{RETRIEVED\_AS\_CANDIDATE}, not as
a resemblance verdict: an edge records only that two works produced at least one
passage pair above the retrieval threshold under one fixed vector-space model.
Known translation, anthology, compilation and retelling relationships are marked
and separated from the discovery ranking. An unmarked pair means ``not covered by
the current dependence rules'', not ``proved independent''. No
\field{DESCENDS\_FROM} or \field{BORROWS\_FROM} edge is inferred from cosine
similarity.

Graph output is split into five layers so that premises, measurements and claims
cannot be confused: documented source dependence between works; lexical retrieval
candidates; structural comparison of annotated narrative versions
(\field{COMPARED\_WITH}); adjudicated historical relationships, which this build
leaves empty; and historical context attributes such as dating, language and
access cautions. Every edge carries its own \field{edge\_kind}, and a validator
rejects a graph in which a retrieval edge has been relabelled as a resemblance,
in which a comparison has been published as a verdict, or in which an
adjudicated relation appears without adjudication. \field{RESEMBLES} is
deliberately reserved: the builder records a promotion criterion ---
independently attested rather than merely unmarked witnesses, a shared comparison
class, every nonzero component sitting on an assessed domain, and survival under
the parameter sweeps --- and no pair in this build satisfies it, so the published
graph contains zero \field{RESEMBLES} edges.

Equation~\eqref{eq:composite} is \textbf{not computed}. Its components are now
computed separately (Section~\ref{sec:structural}), and that is precisely why the
composite is abandoned: the components disagree, sometimes violently, and
averaging them would decide the argument in advance instead of reporting it.
Historical confidence remains a separate ordinal evidence assessment, never a
transformation of a similarity formula.

\subsection{Cleaned global corpus and dependence filtering}

The 42-work baseline was replaced by a cleaned, broader snapshot: 65 works across
14 region labels and 20 source roles, 9{,}568{,}271 source words, of which
8{,}731{,}101 words in 23{,}385 eligible passages entered retrieval. Front matter,
footnotes, appendices, editorial apparatus and passages below the minimum length
were excluded before indexing rather than filtered afterwards.

Retrieval produced 9{,}428 cross-work candidate pairs. \textbf{8{,}677 of them
(92.0\%) join two works already linked by a configured source-dependence rule},
leaving 751 pairs not known dependent. That ratio is the most consequential
number in the lexical experiment: at this corpus size, near-verbatim English
wording is overwhelmingly an artifact of reuse, repetition and shared editions,
not a signal waiting to be interpreted. Random cross-work pairs (\(n = 20{,}000\))
had mean similarity 0.0121 and 99th percentile 0.0416; length-matched pairs (mean
absolute word-count difference 11.8) reached 0.0126 and 0.0426. Known-overlap
positive controls behaved as expected, scoring 0.6000 (Qur'an text shared between
two anthology volumes) and 0.6166 (Dhammapada text repeated in \emph{Sacred Books
of the East}).

A same-corpus ablation quantifies what the cleaning step removes. Without
apparatus and length exclusion the identical passage set yields 29{,}048 candidate
pairs, against 9{,}428 in the published cleaned run, a 67.5\% reduction; because
each configuration refits its own vectorizer, part of that reduction is
vocabulary, IDF and neighbour-cap behaviour rather than exclusion, and holding
the unfiltered fit fixed leaves 10{,}689 pairs between eligible passages. The two
runs are not nested, so the decomposition is reported as approximate. Cleaning
also has a measured cost: seven of the 95 quoted evidence fragments in the
annotation set --- the Popol Vuh wooden-people events and two Ymir body events ---
fall on passages the cleaning rules exclude. The narratives are in the corpus,
but retrieval cannot reach the windows the coder actually cited, so cleaning must
be described as reducing candidates sharply while its narrative recall remains
unvalidated. Order diagnostics are unchanged in kind from the baseline:
word-shuffled self-comparison retains 0.7125 mean similarity and
sentence-shuffled self-comparison retains 0.9858, while a bigram-only model falls
to 0.1071 under word shuffling. Exploratory keyword counts make the same point
about motif frequency: serpent references appear in 2{,}296 passages and
storm/lightning in 2{,}033, but creation-from-body in only 16 and water-release in
4. Distributional inference is impossible for the rare tags at this corpus size.

The 9{,}428 passage pairs aggregate to 201 work pairs, 152 of which are not known
dependent. The lexical graph exposes 60 work nodes and 149 edges: the top 100
not-known-dependent work pairs plus all 49 configured-dependence controls, so the
picture is a sampled triage list rather than a complete similarity map.

What the strongest surviving candidates actually are matters more than their
count. The highest-ranked pair not known dependent, at 0.5166, joins
\emph{Myths of the Cherokee} and \emph{Creek Migration Legends}; manual inspection
found a repeated deer-hunting song opposite Hitchiti dialect material, not a
shared event sequence. Second-ranked at 0.4994 are two Lewis Spence title pages.
Third at 0.4874 pairs two Australian ethnographic works. Ninth, at 0.3123, rests
on a \emph{single} passage pair between an Iroquois collection and a South
African folklore volume --- an artifact of the maximum in
\(R_{\mathrm{work}}(A,B)\), which rewards long works and treats one incidental
coincidence as a work-level claim. The candidate set also contains same-tradition
pairs that no dependence rule covers, such as the \emph{Poetic Edda} and
\emph{Volsunga saga} (122 passage pairs, 0.3886), where similarity is real and
historically uninteresting. After 92\% of raw candidates were removed as known
reuse, what remained was still dominated by apparatus, formulaic material,
collector overlap and single-pair noise. That is the empirical case for treating
lexical retrieval as candidate generation only.

Every raw file hash, every byte-exact clean slice, all 34{,}281 passage offsets
and word counts, all dependence labels and all graph endpoints and orientations
are re-verified by \texttt{tools/validate\_results\_v2.py}, which also refuses to
certify a graph built from stale inputs. Re-running the retrieval reproduced the
9{,}428-pair candidate file byte-for-byte, so the published ranking is a property
of the configuration rather than of one execution.

Two consequences are stated here because the earlier draft left them implicit. First, the
descriptive anchors and the measurements rest on different texts: the coded Zeus--Typhon
version is Evelyn-White's 1914 Hesiod rather than West's edition, the Thor versions are
Anderson's \emph{Younger Edda} rather than Faulkes or Larrington, the Ymir version is

quotes, and the flood tablet is Smith's \emph{Chaldean Account of Genesis} rather than
George's critical edition. Where the corpus holds no rendering at all --- the two
Illuyanka versions, the \emph{Rigveda} 1.32 hymn --- nothing was coded and no score exists
for it. Second, the fix is not to cite better editions beside scores computed from worse
ones: it is to re-read each version in the edition the anchor names, re-code it, and
re-run. The coding does record which of its own events rest on secondary literature ---
\texttt{ymir\_body}'s killing event is coded to Bellows' \emph{note} about a lost
narrative, and its \texttt{attestation\_note} says so --- which is the mechanism a
re-coding pass needs in order to know which events are exposed when the text is read
again.

Similarity is decomposed rather than blended. An event pair receives
\begin{equation}
  e(a,b) = \frac{0.50\,p(a,b) + 0.30\,r(a,b) + 0.20\,o(a,b)}
               {\text{weights of measurable parts}},
\end{equation}
where \(p\) credits predicate identity (1.0 exact, 0.5 same predicate family, 0
otherwise), \(r\) is a maximum-weight bipartite matching over participants scored
on the coder's asserted referent identity, normalised by the larger participant
set, and \(o\) is lexical overlap of coded outcomes. A part that cannot be
measured on both sides drops out of the average instead of contributing a zero, so
that incomplete coding is not read as narrative difference. Event sequences are
then aligned by Needleman--Wunsch with mismatch cost \(1-e(a,b)\) and the
preregistered gap \(g = 1.0\), giving
\begin{equation}
  S_{\mathrm{sequence}} = 1 - \frac{D_{mn}}{\max(m,n)} .
\end{equation}
Motif comparison is a weighted Jaccard restricted to the assessed domain: both
versions must have adjudicated the motif. Cross-class pairs return \field{NA},
not a low score. Causal, role and symbol components are computed the same way,
and the symbol component reports which of its matched terms were already counted
as motifs so that double counting is visible rather than silent.

Four findings follow.

\textbf{Dependent witnesses separate cleanly from unrelated pairs.} Both
known-reuse pairs outrank every predicted-unrelated pair on
\(S_{\mathrm{sequence}}\) (minimum 0.632 against maximum 0.045), and the
Mahabharata internal doublet anchors the scale at 1.000 across all five
components. The Taliesin pair is more informative than a perfect control: the
abridged \emph{Celtic Myths} rendering drops three drops of grace, the year and a
day and the blind fire-tender, and the motif domain falls from 11 to 5 while the
event skeleton still aligns at 0.632. The engine measures what the retelling
actually kept.

\textbf{Translation vocabulary does not enter the score.} Re-coding every
participant name with a random alias changes \(S_{\mathrm{sequence}}\) by 0.0 on
all 91 pairs, because matching reads the asserted referent, not the translator's
word. This is a property no lexical model has: the same rename destroys TF-IDF
similarity. It also means the engine will not equate Thor with Indra merely
because both are storm gods; they are different asserted referents, and only role
and kind structure is shared.

\textbf{Event order, as preregistered, barely contributes.} Permuting one
sequence lowers \(S_{\mathrm{sequence}}\) by only 0.0186 on average, below the
0.02 floor fixed in advance, and just 5 of 91 pairs exceed \(p \le 0.05\) against
a 200-draw permutation null. The cause is arithmetic rather than a property of
myth: with mismatch cost \(1-e \in [0,1]\) and \(g = 1.0\), aligning is never more
expensive than gapping both events, so 91 of 91 pairs match every event of the
shorter sequence and the alignment behaves like a maximum-weight order-preserving
matching. Measured directly against the crossing relaxation, order costs 0.0131
on average and 0.101 at most, and 77 of 91 pairs pay something for it. The honest
conclusion is that \(S_{\mathrm{sequence}}\) is an \emph{event-matching} score,
not a plot-order score. Restoring order sensitivity would mean lowering the gap,
which imports a free parameter, moves the ranking (mean absolute rank shift
0.23--0.29 across the gap sweep) and changes what the measure means. The result is
reported instead of tuned, and the motif component is exactly invariant under the
same permutations, as the decomposition requires.

\textbf{Structure and wording carry different information.} Across the 78 pairs
with both measures, Spearman \(\rho = 0.264\) between
\(S_{\mathrm{sequence}}\) and the lexical cosine, which is a maximum over the
passage windows the annotation actually cites for the two versions rather than a
whole-work maximum, reported as an exploratory association rather than a
significant one. The textbook \(p = 0.019\) assumed 78 independent pair
observations, which they are not: the 91 pairs share 14 versions, so every
version contributes 13 correlated scores. A quadratic-assignment permutation test
that holds the pair structure fixed and resamples only the version labels
(200 draws, seed recorded in the output) gives two-tailed \(p = 0.065\). The
direction of the association is what this pilot can support; its magnitude is not
established, and no significance claim attaches to it. Noah--Manu scores 0.243
structurally against 0.0358 lexically; Noah--Gilgamesh scores 0.550 on
adjudicated motifs over a domain of 4 against 0.095 on events; and Noah--Popol
Vuh produces a structural comparison at all only because the annotation, not the
retriever, brought them together. A ranking built on either measure alone would
misrepresent the other.

Robustness checks are correspondingly modest. Rank movement is reported as mean
absolute rank displacement divided by the full ranking range, so 0 is the
published order and 1 is its exact reversal, with Kendall \(\tau\) alongside.
Judgement weights barely move anything: the same-family predicate credit sweep
gives shifts of 0.001--0.011 (\(\tau \ge 0.975\)) and the corpus-derived
leave-one-region-out weights at most 0.017. The motif weight cap sweep moves at
most 0.013 (\(\tau \ge 0.973\)). The gap penalty is another matter: 0.291 at
\(g = 0.5\) (\(\tau = 0.106\)), 0.262 at 0.75, 0.231 at 1.25 and 0.284 at 1.5,
against 0.001 at the preregistered \(g = 1.0\). The ordering is therefore
parameter-bound on the alignment settings, not on the analyst's weights --- the
more worrying kind of dependence, because the alignment is where the measure's
meaning is decided. A thin-domain audit flags 3 pairs whose motif score rests on
a single adjudicated motif and 8 on two or fewer; those are reported as computed
but should not be read as agreement. The engine emits no composite and no
historical claim, and 43 structural-engine tests enforce that.

The limits of this pilot are as important as its results. All 14 versions were
coded by a single analyst, so \nocr, and the predicate families, motif
definitions and coding decisions were authored by the same person who applied
them. Quote verification proves that a fragment occurs in the cited passage; it
does not prove the coding is correct. Four classes and 14 versions cannot support
any distributional claim about world narrative, and the corpus they draw on is
English translation. The engine is a working instrument for measuring descriptive
resemblance between coded narratives, and a test of which assumptions a
structural claim can rest on. It is not, and does not claim to be, an ancestry
estimator.

\section{The divine-concept question}

\subsection{The motive claim, stated so that it can fail}
\label{sec:motive}

This project was commissioned with a stronger hope than the analyses above test:
that ``God'' is one invented concept --- a single fiction that spread, rather
than a response to anything real --- and that its apparent worldwide recurrence is
the evidence of its invention. That sentence contains three propositions with
three different epistemic statuses, and separating them is the condition of
testing them rather than a refusal to test them.

\textbf{Proposition A: divine narratives descend from a limited number of
ancestral formulations.} This is a historical claim and is testable with the
instruments in this paper. Its falsifiers are the ones the protocol
preregisters: a shared \emph{specific} complex (arbitrary, non-functional detail,
not merely a shared theme) distributed along a dated contact chain; phylogenetic
signal that survives a regional-null comparison; and the absence of the same
complex from the earliest strata of families that had no contact route. Its
disconfirmers are equally concrete: independent attestation in lineages with no
plausible route, distribution patterns better predicted by convergence, and
shared features that turn out to be artifacts of the translators' English rather
than of the source texts.

\textbf{Proposition B: the concept itself, not only its narratives, has a single
origin.} This requires evidence that similarity scoring cannot supply: dated
first attestations of each divine-concept state, the transmission of
\emph{doctrinal} vocabulary rather than plot, and an account of how a concept
survives translation between languages whose grammatical subject positions
differ. It is a legitimate research question for philology and the history of
religion; this project's apparatus is not yet addressed to it.

\textbf{Proposition C: no such beings exist.} No textual comparison can
establish this or its negation. An idea's origin tells you nothing about its
truth: arguing from ``this concept was narrated by humans'' to ``therefore it
refers to nothing'' is a genetic fallacy, and the reverse inference is no better.
A project designed to prove C would necessarily read the same evidence as
confirming when it favours the preferred conclusion, which is precisely the
unfalsifiability condemned above. C is therefore excluded from this project's
scope by declaration, not by dismissal.

Proposition B has a precondition that nobody in this research tradition appears
to have tested computationally, and this project tested it: that the word
\emph{god} picks out one thing across the traditions being compared.

\subsection{The vocabulary census and the withdrawal of its inference}

The first probe, \texttt{tools/divinity\_terms.py}, matched ten
analyst-authored divine-concept grammars --- personal creator of world,
supreme-being attributes, plural or council deities, celestial bureaucracy,
impersonal ground, deified human or ancestor, spirit immanent in things, saving
descent, explicit non-theism or scepticism, and exclusivist monotheism --- against
the 8.73 million eligible words of the same 65-work corpus, recording every match
with its passage and character span. The descriptive distribution stands and is
reported here: no grammar is attested in all 65 works. The broadest is
divinity-as-plurality (4{,}120 term hits, 50 works, 13 of 14 regional labels),
followed by the personal creator (728 hits, 41 works) and supreme-being attributes
(802 hits, 35 works); the impersonal absolute appears in 26 works, the
deified-human grammar in 14, and heaven-as-bureaucracy in 3 works with 4 hits.
Sixty-one of the 65 works carry at least one grammar and four carry none; among the
sixty-one the count per work runs from one to eight.

\textbf{What that distribution does not establish.} It establishes that the
English vocabulary of these translations is unevenly distributed across several
distinguishable concepts. It does not establish that ``the concept of God'' has no
stable referent, and it does not weaken an inheritance hypothesis, for two
reasons the earlier draft did not face. First, the test chosen --- must one
grammar occur in \emph{every} work? --- is a universality test, and universality
is not what historical inheritance predicts. Inheritance predicts descent with
modification, and the modifications are themselves named: abstraction turns a
personal creator into an impersonal ground, demotion turns a god into a demon or
an ancestor, nominalisation turns a title into a proper name, silencing removes a
term in a recension. A hypothesis that expects transformation expects the surface
forms to diverge, so demanding that they not diverge refutes a straw version of
it. Second, and more fundamentally, every instrument here detects an English
surface pattern in one translation. Non-detection licenses exactly one report ---
``not detected in these sources'' --- and all four competing models (single origin
with transformation, multiple independent origins, regional inheritance with
mixing, independent development with convergence) predict that same
non-detection pattern. An observation that all models predict cannot rank any of
them. The census is therefore a description of translators' vocabulary, not
evidence about ancestry, and the earlier sentence drawing a negative conclusion
about common origin from it is withdrawn.

The 34 is the largest set any independence sentence may be about, and it is still
labelled \emph{not marked dependent} rather than \emph{independent}.
\texttt{config/source\_dependence.json} lists pair rules one at a time; there is no
closure over the corpus, still less over the external sources each work reprints, and
the Sale--Rodwell pair is the proof: the artifact's
\texttt{verbatim\_overlap\_with\_declared\_source} is false for it, because two
renderings of one composition need not share a wording, so a rule whose text does not
recur detects a source-level dependence it cannot localise, and two works can be
independent printings of one non-corpus source while no rule mentions either. The
record field keeps its v1 name \texttt{counts\_as\_independent\_attestation} only so
the schema stays stable; the artifact publishes what it means, under
\texttt{verdict\_counts.matches\_by\_dependence\_marking}.

What remains after the audit is still real and still worth stating: exclusivist
oneness is unevenly distributed across this corpus, concentrated in Abrahamic
textual families, and absent from most works in it. But it is now a
\emph{descriptive} observation about a partly-audited vocabulary census, not a
demonstration that the doctrine is confined to one family, and not a refutation of
anything. Texts that deny or suspend gods are also present --- 116 machine matches
in 20 works across ten tradition labels, published with locators and marked
\field{unreviewed\_machine\_match} --- though their reading requires care, since a
conditional in the \emph{Iliad}, the doxographic ``atheists'' of the Mahabharata,
and Isaiah's ``beside me there is no God'' are three different speech acts.
Forty-one monotheistic-formula matches were excluded from that family by a
published rule and are printed rather than discarded, because a regex that cannot
tell confession from denial must not be allowed to silently choose.

\subsection{The rebuilt state coder: scale, coverage and refusal}
\label{sec:coder}

The replacement for the census is an instrument that codes divine-concept
\emph{states}, and every one of its design decisions came from an external audit
of the first version rather than from a result it produced. Seven properties
matter for reading any number it publishes.

\textbf{The unit is a proposition, not a passage.} Each utterance window is split
at coordinating and subordinating connectors; fragments under three words merge
into the preceding proposition; a match straddling a boundary widens its
proposition instead of being attributed to the wrong half. The run emits 47{,}641
records over 36{,}775 propositions in 34{,}584 windows within the same 23{,}385
eligible passages. Fifty-nine distinct propositions carry the probable run-on flag, and they
write \(97\) records, because the flag belongs to a proposition while a proposition records
one asserted value per row; the run summary publishes both figures under
\field{propositions\_flagged\_run\_on} and \field{records\_on\_flagged\_propositions}. The unit key
is \((\text{work},\text{passage},\text{proposition start})\), and on
\(13\) of those \(36{,}775\) units two matches at the same start widen to different ends,
so a proposition does not always have one recorded span and the count of distinct
\((\text{start},\text{end})\) pairs is \(36{,}788\); the \(13\) sit in the two Qur'an
volumes, and twelve of them are the confession formula, where the denial of other gods ends
the proposition at \texttt{There is no God} while the exclusivity match widens it to
\texttt{no God but He, the Compassionate, the Merciful}. The thirteenth is not that formula at
all: a \dimkey{D6} pair widens \texttt{banished, once} to \texttt{once for all}, which is the
same mechanism on another dimension. The number the paper quotes is the number of units, not of spans,
and \texttt{tests/test\_divinity\_ontology.py} re-derives both from the shipped record file so the
difference cannot be silently absorbed into a rounded count.
Where the machine would have to choose between two values on a single-valued
dimension it now publishes the conflict and demotes the record rather than
resolving it by silent precedence: 2 conflicts on acknowledged population, 3 on
speaker attribution. The segmentation is a machine approximation with four
documented failure modes, one of them that Hebrew and Old Norse narrative joins
clauses with no connector at all; a coder who re-segments a proposition produces
a measurement of the segmentation, not an error to be corrected in the record.

\textbf{The composite dimensions were split} (Table~\ref{tab:dims}). Plurality
became three questions, because one sentence can acknowledge other gods, treat
them as real and forbid worshipping them, and a single ``polytheism'' score erased
exactly that distinction. Human interpretation became four, because a hymn can
carry doctrine, a conditional can carry reported speech, and an opponent can make
a theological argument.

\textbf{Scale is published as matrices, with a floor on interpretation.} The
passage-by-value matrix holds 23{,}385 rows against 50 value columns,
1{,}169{,}250 cells; the work-by-dimension table holds 780 cells and carries the

doctrinal tally can never exceed the candidates it is drawn from. A dark cell is
not an absence in a tradition: it is either no candidate in this one English
translation or a value the instrument cannot see, and those are different
failures.

\textbf{Candidate generation was an approximation, and is now exact}
(Table~\ref{tab:graph}). The first release of this layer blocked candidate pairs
with MinHash banding, wrote every pair the banding produced, and reported that as
completeness because nothing was discarded \emph{after} generation. A validator
measured the blocking step and found it recovered 2.35\% of the pairs it claimed to
enumerate---so the published pair set was a small sample of the relation it
described. The layer now ships the thing the sketch was approximating: an exact
inverted index over live \field{dimension=value} states, joined posting-list by
posting-list, work-pair by work-pair. The relation \emph{shares at least one live
coded state with} is therefore enumerated rather than sampled, its counts are
published whole, and the index itself ships as
\texttt{results/ontology\_passage\_index.json.gz} so a reader holding that one file
can regenerate every pair without this code.

\texttt{minhash\_signatures} and \texttt{band\_candidates}, so it measures the

\textbf{The enumeration is exact for one relation and silent about everything else.}
What the index joins is states this instrument wrote down. 40 of the frame's 93
declared values carry no English surface pattern and 9 are labelled
\field{not\_yet\_instrumented}, so a pair absent from the enumeration either really
shares no coded state or shares a state the detector cannot see, and the artifact
cannot tell which from inside. The edge file is additionally a declared display
slice---the top 20 pairs of each connected work pair by shared-state count---so its
size is a property of the reader's screen, not of the relation. \textbf{A missing
edge is therefore never a missing relationship or a missing state}, and that rule is
what the exact index buys: it converts ``we did not look at that pair'' into ``that
pair shares nothing we can see,'' which is a weaker claim but a checkable one. Layer
4 remains empty because dates are not data yet.

\textbf{The interpretation dimensions are drawn on their own denominator.}
D7a--d are decided from the shape of an utterance window before any divine term is
read, so a percentage on them is not comparable to a candidate count on a concept
dimension. The figure set gives the four of them separate panels, a separate
denominator of the 47{,}641 term-carrying records and their own legend, with a
caption saying so, plus a second heat map that uses only affirmative-doctrine
counts. The earlier chart had substituted a count of windows-read-as-doctrine into
a heat map of matched candidates, which is the error this design exists to avoid
repeating.

\textbf{The frame is multilingual-ready, not multilingual.} Its dimensions,
values, operator vocabulary and evidence requirements are stated independently of
English, and every record carries an \field{expression\_language} field set to
\texttt{en}; but all detectors in this run are English translation-text patterns,
and no source-language detector is operational. Describing it as a
language-independent instrument would claim the property the audit said it lacks.

\textbf{No number here licenses a historical claim, and that is its point.} The
instrument says how many propositions carry which coded state in one generation of
English translations, which of those the text itself endorses, which values it
cannot see, and what fraction of the pairs it could have compared it actually did.

\subsection{The annotation packet: built, blinded, unused}

A blinded packet of 6{,}199 propositions is ready for coding: 4{,}699 that the
detector flagged and 1{,}500 that it flagged as nothing. Table~\ref{tab:packet}
states its design and its single binding constraint. The second group is the
change that matters for what can be claimed. A sample drawn only from hits can
measure precision and cannot measure recall, because a sample of hits contains no
misses; and \texttt{not\_yet\_instrumented} is a declared gap on a named value, not
an estimate of what the detector walked past. So the packet now draws two disjoint
simple random samples from the propositions the detector asserted nothing about --- the
complement of its detection set, which includes the \(441\) propositions that carry a
record it declined to treat as a detection --- and
every probe row carries its stratum's exact inclusion probability and weight.

The scorer exists, computes Cohen's \(\kappa\) for single-valued dimensions and
mean Jaccard for set-valued ones, joins the answer key only after both sheets are
supplied, and refuses to run on one coder or on the same sheet supplied twice. It
now also refuses a key whose probe rows carry no weight, and it keeps the two
estimands in separate blocks: per-value precision is computed on machine-flagged
rows only, and detector recall is a Horvitz--Thompson estimate,
\(\text{tp}_v / (\text{tp}_v + \sum w / c)\), where \(\text{tp}_v\) is
\(\text{detected}_v\) multiplied by the share of flagged propositions both coders
kept on a candidate row, the miss sum runs over
probe rows \emph{both} coders independently read as carrying a divine claim, \(c\)
is the share of the drawn stratum's weight that was actually coded, and
\(\text{detected}_v\) is counted over the whole corpus rather than over the packet,
over records whose \texttt{assertion\_state} is \texttt{candidate} and no other.
A \texttt{not\_yet\_instrumented} record announces that no detector for the value
exists and a \texttt{candidate\_requires\_human\_disambiguation} record names a value
the detector declined to choose; counting either as a detection gave five values a
published recall for an instrument that was never written, and removed their
propositions from the frame where a miss could be found. The uncorrected
\(\text{detected}_v / (\text{detected}_v + \sum w)\) is published beside the
discounted figure as an upper bound, so the size of the correction is visible.
A stratum that finds no miss reports a one-sided exact binomial bound on its miss
share instead of a recall of one. No human has coded the packet. Consequently no
reliability artifact exists in this repository, and this paper reports no
\(\kappa\), no precision and no recall for the coder-facing instrument. Machine
classifications in Table~\ref{tab:states} are therefore candidates, not gold
standard.

\subsection{The instrument measured against its own chance line}
\label{sec:anomalies}

A concordance number means nothing until something is subtracted from it. Two works
that both print \field{devotion\_mediated\_by\_hierarchy} agree, but only the second
one is interesting if that value is not simply what this corpus always prints --- and
here it is, carrying \(0.893\) of the \dimkey{D2c} value tokens and setting that
dimension's own baseline at \(0.632\). So
\texttt{tools/scan\_dimension\_anomalies.py} measures every dimension against a
baseline computed from the corpus's own value frequencies and publishes
\texttt{results/dimension\_anomalies.json}. The null is a per-dimension configuration
model: each synthetic work draws exactly as many values as the real work it replaces,
weighted by that dimension's observed prevalence, and the synthetic pairs are then
scored with the statistic the frame declares --- exact match on a single-valued key,
Jaccard on a set-valued one. No permutation control is offered, and none exists to
offer: the mean over a complete graph is invariant to permuting which work owns which
set, so a label-shuffled ``control'' would return the observed number and call it a
null. Handing each synthetic work another work's \emph{whole} value set is degenerate the
same way, and there the word is exact rather than rhetorical: measured dimension by
dimension, the expectation of that resample equals the observed complete-graph mean, so it
reproduces the data instead of bounding it. The pair set is the one tightening available
without collapsing, and it is applied: the line under every scored-graph number in
Table~\ref{tab:chance} is drawn over the same pairs as the mean above it. The draw is seeded (\texttt{seed} \(20261102\), \(200\) repetitions) and, after
a run-to-run drift of \(10^{-4}\) was traced to iteration order, sorted twice ---
marginals by value, carriers by work id --- so the artifact is byte-reproducible under
any \texttt{PYTHONHASHSEED}.

Table~\ref{tab:chance} is the result. It is measured on the pair graph the comparison
layer actually scores, which is a subset of the corpus: of \(2080\) possible pairs of
the \(65\) works, \texttt{similarity\_between} refuses any pair below \(6\) comparable
keys, leaving \(1237\) pairs over \(52\) works.

\textbf{Seven findings, none of which is a claim about the traditions.}

\textbf{(1) The frame is a nine-key instrument on this corpus.} \dimkey{D7b\_speech\_act},
\dimkey{D7c\_discourse\_genre} and \dimkey{D7d\_epistemic\_commitment} fired on \(0\) of
the \(65\) works. They are the three discourse keys whose absence this layer had already
conceded in prose; here the concession is measured. The consequence is asymmetric: the
\texttt{arbitrary\_or\_expressive} block, which M4 predicts to be the \emph{least}
concordant, is left with two live keys of the five it declares, and one of those two sits
inside the chance band, so the block is carried by a single dimension.

\textbf{(2) \dimkey{D7a\_speaker\_attribution} does not clear its own base rate.} Observed
agreement is \(0.510\), which reads like a coin flip; the chance line computed from its
own marginals is \(0.504\), so the excess is \(+0.006\), and under the stricter endorsement
frame --- the same dimension counted only where the text's own voice affirms it --- the
excess is \(-0.006\), which is not a direction. A dimension this flat cannot support the
claim that ``who is speaking'' is a separable axis of this corpus, and the D7 panel in
\texttt{results/track\_b\_state.html} should be read as a display of one value's
prevalence, not of an interpretive structure.

\textbf{(3) The declared statistic has no defined answer on most of the cells it is applied
to.} The frame declares \dimkey{D2a}, \dimkey{D2b}, \dimkey{D2c} and \dimkey{D7a}
single-valued, yet \(89\) of the \(157\) works that carry one of those four keys carry
\(>1\) value, so exact match is undefined on \(0.567\) of the live single-valued cells. The
comparison layer now treats such a cell as a set and publishes the count of offenders per
dimension rather than choosing silently between two readings. Both readings are in the
artifact, and on \dimkey{D7a} they say different things: over the same \(893\) pairs the
declared exact-match rule reports \(0.510\) against a chance line of \(0.504\), while the set
reading reports \(0.733\) against \(0.728\). The two disagree by \(0.224\) about how
concordant this corpus is and by only \(0.0004\) about how much of that is above chance, so
the choice of statistic moves the level the argument is made about without moving the
finding --- which is what makes the excess, not the observed mean, the reportable quantity.

\textbf{(4) The pair population you choose moves the numbers, so the baseline has to be
drawn on it too.} A work rich enough to be scored at all is not a random work, so the scored
graph and the carrier graph give different means: \dimkey{D4\_cosmic\_function} moves
\(0.0988\) between them, which is larger than the \(0.05\) this scan uses to call an excess
indistinguishable from chance. A subtraction owes that discipline to both of its terms, so
the scored graph's chance line is drawn over its own \(1237\) pairs rather than over every
pair of the \(52\) works those pairs cover, and the artifact publishes both lines next to
the gap between them. Conditioning moves no chance line by more than \(0.0098\) --- a fifth
of the band --- and changes \(2\) of the \(18\) band verdicts, both of them
\dimkey{D6\_temporal\_nature}'s, which crosses from outside the band to inside it.

That crossing is not cosmetic. Dropping the dimensions inside the chance band from the block
means --- an operation whose only content is ``the instrument cannot separate these values''
--- turns on whether \dimkey{D6} is dropped, and with the line drawn on the scored pairs it
is. The declared adjacency \texttt{structural \(>\) arbitrary} is then a \emph{tie} on both
graphs (\(-0.041\) carrier, \(-0.045\) scored), and no adjacency verdict changes between the
two graphs at all. Same corpus, same statistic, same registered separability, one arithmetic
choice about which pairs to count --- and one about which pairs the baseline was counted
over.

\textbf{(5) The falsification hook that was registered against M4 fires on the raw scale and
not on the corrected one, so it fires on the instrument.} M4's alpha vector predicts
\texttt{functional \(>\) structural \(>\) arbitrary}. On the \(1237\) pairs the gate reads,
raw mean similarity gives \(0.636\), \(0.365\), \(0.542\): the middle block is the
\emph{lowest}, a reversal of \(-0.177\), which is the committed
\texttt{block\_ordering\_contradicts\_declared\_propensity} verdict, and under the version of
this test registered before the chance line existed it was the verdict the corpus produced.
Subtract each dimension's own baseline and the same three block means become \(0.118\),
\(0.041\), \(0.058\): the reversal is \(-0.017\), which is inside the
\(0.05\) separability this layer itself declares in \field{minimum\_block\_spread}, whose
recorded basis already says ``a reversal smaller than it is a tie, not a refutation.'' The
test now publishes both readings and, because they straddle the disqualification line, issues
\texttt{block\_profile\_verdict\_depends\_on\_the\_similarity\_scale} and disqualifies
nothing. That is the honest outcome in both directions: it does not let a base-rate artefact
strip M4's name, and it does not let M4 be scored as confirmed by a profile that reverses on
whichever scale the analyst happened to subtract.

\textbf{(6) The pairs that look like transmission are the pairs that are the same book.}
\(550\) of the \(1237\) scored pairs show the pattern M4 predicts to be rare --- the
arbitrary block out-agreeing the functional one --- and every one of the top \(15\) pairs by
excess agreement above chance is flagged dependent: \texttt{mahabharata\_v3} against
\texttt{mahabharata\_v4}, \texttt{cherokee\_myths} against \texttt{chinese\_folklore},
\texttt{myths\_legends\_china} against \texttt{myths\_mexico\_peru}. Dependence is published
per pair and never filtered away, because filtering would delete the very structure the
ranking is confusing for signal.

\textbf{(7) The chance band is not a test level, and measured against its own null it is the
wrong size for most of this panel.} The \(0.05\) that separates ``indistinguishable from
chance'' from ``anomalous'' was set by the analyst and never calibrated, so the scan now
redraws the null \(200\) times per measurement and reports how far a pure-noise corpus
travels from its own chance line (leave-one-out, so no draw is compared to a line it helped
draw). On the scored graph the band's own false-positive rate ranges from \(0.000\) ---
\(10\) of the \(18\) measurements never leave it --- to \(0.555\) on
\dimkey{D2c\_worship\_exclusivity}: for that one dimension, a corpus with no structure in it
crosses the band more often than not, because its null scatter has a standard deviation of
\(0.0845\) against a band half that wide, while \dimkey{D4\_cosmic\_function} wanders by
\(0.0045\). Taken as a family, a noise-only corpus of this size puts at
least one measurement outside the band in \(0.760\) of draws, a mean of \(1.01\) measurements.
The same graph flags \(5\) of its \(9\) live dimensions on the declared statistic against a
noise-only expectation of \(0.80\), so the count is not nothing; what it cannot do is certify
any single one of the five, because the band that produced it fires on
\dimkey{D2c\_worship\_exclusivity} more often than a coin.
The per-dimension Monte Carlo reading is the sharper one, and it disagrees with the band on
three dimensions, all in the same direction: \dimkey{D2a\_acknowledged\_population}
(\(+0.021\)) and \dimkey{D2b\_reality\_of\_others} (\(+0.030\)) sit \emph{inside} the band yet
clear their own null at \(p \le 0.05\), because those two dimensions wander by only \(0.0065\)
and \(0.0135\), and \dimkey{D6\_temporal\_nature} (\(+0.048\)) joins them inside the band by two
thousandths while sitting at the floor of its own null. The dimension the band used to over-call
is the one that moved: \dimkey{D2c\_worship\_exclusivity}, which the band calls anomalous at
\(+0.148\), now clears it (\(p = 0.025\)) --- but on a measurement whose null scatter is
\(0.0845\), whose band false-positive rate is \(0.555\) and whose \(p\) is \(5\) draws in \(201\),
that is the least trustworthy reading in the panel, not the strongest. Six dimensions --- \dimkey{D1\_ontological\_category},
\dimkey{D2a\_acknowledged\_population}, \dimkey{D3\_agency}, \dimkey{D4\_cosmic\_function},
\dimkey{D5\_universe\_relationship}, \dimkey{D6\_temporal\_nature} --- survive a Bonferroni cut
at \(0.05/9 = 0.005556\) on the declared-rule reading, and every one of them sits at the floor
this many draws can report (\(p = 1/201 = 0.004975\), the smallest \(p\) \(200\) replicates can
express). The published
conclusion is therefore the corrected one: this instrument separates six dimensions from their
own base rate, the single absolute band is the wrong summary for a panel whose null widths
span an order of magnitude, and no \(\alpha\) here is family-wise-corrected across the two
readings, the two graphs and the endorsement-frame sensitivity that are also published.

What this section is not: the null preserves each work's own number of values, each
dimension's prevalence and the pair set the observed mean was measured on, and it does not
preserve correlations \emph{between} values inside a work, so every excess here is an upper bound
on what structure could be attributed to that dimension; the classification is detector output over English translations, awaiting the
human coding that has not happened; and with zero dated attestations filled, none of these
numbers can be read as transmission in either direction.

\section{The four competing models and the licence conditions on preferring one}
\label{sec:models}

The working hypothesis is \emph{inheritance with transformation}: concepts are
transmitted and then modified, so the descendant state need not resemble the
ancestor. This is not unfalsifiable, because it does not predict arbitrary
outcomes --- it predicts states reachable by named operators along typed, dated
routes. Four models are specified, and each is a likelihood rather than a mood.
Table~\ref{tab:models} states their admissions, because a model that admits
everything explains nothing.

\textbf{Two edge types, and the confession that the first version conflated
them.} Descent through a cultural or population lineage (vertical inheritance) and
movement by contact (horizontal transmission: borrowing, back-borrowing,
deliberate restoration, revival, translation-mediated transference, coercive
religiation, editorial citation) are different claims with different evidence. The
conflation was real, not hypothetical: the first version of this layer carried no
edge type at all and computed a distance gradient over an undifferentiated
relation, so a short-distance contact pair could score like a long-distance
descent pair, and M1's parsimony was inflated by exactly the amount the borrowing
edges contribute --- which is the thing M3 exists to compete with. So
\field{edge\_type} is now mandatory and exclusive; \texttt{descent\_or\_contact}
is an illegal value; an edge with a documented contact route that could carry the
state alone is reported \field{edge\_type\_ambiguous} and scored under both
typings or not at all. And a descent claim may not be argued from the similarity
it is supposed to explain: the tool refuses descent evidence whose declared basis
is \texttt{conceptual\_similarity}, because an edge inferred from its own
explanandum makes the hypothesis unfalsifiable by construction.

\textbf{The prediction is a distribution, not a decay curve.} The earlier
prediction that dissimilarity increases monotonically with dated distance from the
root is withdrawn because it is the wrong shape: inherited concepts are
transformed, rediscovered in an archive, borrowed back by the lineage that
exported them, and deliberately restored by reform movements that prefer the older
state. What replaces it is a distribution over similarity class given the pair's
date ranges, its typed edge multiset and its contact status. Each operator \(o\)
fires on each dated edge as a thinned Poisson process with its declared rate
\(\lambda_o\), and each firing multiplies retained similarity by its declared
retention \(\rho_o\), so
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}[\ln s] = \sum_o k_o \ln \rho_o ,
\end{equation}
where the \emph{identity} of the operators sets the rate of change, not the
elapsed years. Horizontal edges carry a declared rate factor of 1.2, and a
restoration, revival or back-borrowing edge resets similarity toward the archived
witness with probability 0.35, capped at 0.8. All of these are analyst-set priors
recorded with their reasons; none is a measurement, and the mean edge event rate
is reported with a sensitivity band of \([0.1, 0.6]\) until data can estimate it.

The old monotone prediction is retained as a \textbf{nested special case} ---
equal rates across operators, one averaged retention (the unweighted geometric
mean of the thirteen, 0.564, at the same total rate of 0.310 events per edge),
reversals switched off --- which is what makes it falsifiable rather than
decorative. The sharpest consequence is that a model with one averaged retention
cannot put any probability on the ``near'' class (\(0.75 \le s < 1.0\)), because a
single factor jumps from 1 straight to \(\rho\). Dated pairs that sit in ``near''
concordance therefore refute the decay submodel outright and are predicted by the
operator-structured one. That is the cheapest observation that discriminates, and
it is the first thing to look for once dates exist.

\textbf{Similarity is a defined quantity.} Exact match on the single-valued keys,
Jaccard on the set-valued ones, mean over comparable keys, \field{undecidable}
keys skipped and published as skipped, and no score below six comparable keys. No
value is defaulted, because a defaulted 0.0 would read as evidence for M2 and a
defaulted 1.0 as evidence for M1.

\textbf{The twelve keys are two kinds of thing, and are not averaged into one
number without saying so.} D1--D6 say what a proposition claims is the case;
D7a--D7d say what kind of speech it is. In a corpus of English translations the
second block is heavily a property of the translator's prose and of genre
convention, so a pair of catechisms can reach a high overall mean while
disagreeing on every conceptual key, and a creation narrative beside a creation
hymn can reach a low one while agreeing on D1, D3, D4 and D6. One mean hides
both cases in the same number, so the scorer publishes, beside the mean, a mean
for each reading block and a \emph{pattern} naming which block moved
(Table~\ref{tab:reading-blocks}). The high/low cut is \(0.5\), the \field{partial}
boundary already declared in the same rule set and reused rather than newly
chosen, so it is the same number the probability grid uses and not a second
threshold tuned for this table. Because the mean is taken over \emph{keys}, the
blocks carry unequal weight by their size: eight of the twelve keys are
conceptual, so a pair disagreeing on all four discourse keys scores \(0.666667\)
while a pair disagreeing on all eight conceptual keys scores \(0.333333\). That
is a consequence of the declared key list, not a finding, and the pattern column
exists so that a reader sees which block moved instead of inferring it from the
mixture.

Three consequences are enforced rather than exhorted. (i) A claimed derivation may
invoke at most \textbf{three} operator applications, at most \textbf{two} if it is
to be reported as preferred without an attested intermediate, and at most
\textbf{one} intermediate node may be unattested. (ii)
\texttt{scope\_narrowing}, \texttt{scope\_expansion}, \texttt{functional\_transfer}
and \texttt{nominalisation} are marked attractor-confusable and may not
constitute a derivation on their own, however cheap they are: independent origin
predicts the same moves, so their cheapness is visibility, not discrimination.
(iii) \texttt{silencing} may be asserted only where the passage is attested on both
sides; it may never be used to explain a state that is simply missing from an
incomplete corpus, because that converts an instrument gap into a historical event.

\textbf{Dates are ranges, and survival is not composition.} Each expression
carries \field{date\_earliest}, \field{date\_latest} and \field{dating\_basis}. An
earlier surviving witness is not necessarily the earlier composition, and the
scaffold makes that concrete for this corpus: of its 65 works, 22 carry an edition
or translation date, 43 require specialist input, and \textbf{0 are usable for a
priority claim}. A translation or printing date constrains when the English
wording existed, not when the concept was held; conflating the two is what let the
first version's undated counts read as chronology.

\textbf{The decision rule is two stages, and its evidence floors are simulated.} A derivation is not preferred
because it is possible. Stage 1 asks which of the four models describes a pair set: all four score the
identical pairs with the identical likelihood function, and the decisive quantity is the leader's
\(\log_{10}\) Bayes factor over the \emph{runner-up}, at a declared 2.0 (100:1). Over the runner-up rather than
over M2, because a rule written as ``inheritance beats the null'' makes M2 the denominator, M4 decorative and
the choice between M1 and M3 untested --- the asymmetry the first version of this rule had.
\texttt{no\_decisive\_winner} is a declared outcome and a published result, not a run that failed to produce
one. Stage 2 keeps the licence question the margin was originally written to answer: an inheritance-bearing
model must beat \emph{both} no-edge, no-operator nulls at the same single declared margin, the licensed quantity
being the smaller of its \(\log_{10}\) Bayes factors over M2 and over M4, with the binding one published per
model --- because the first version of this rule named M4 a second baseline, observed that a margin against M2
alone is too easy, and then applied a margin against M2 alone. Both stages are emitted on every run and neither
implies the other; the layer's own fixture is the demonstration, two inheritance-bearing models predicting one
profile, so stage 1 separates them by exactly 0.0 while stage 2 puts both 226.99 log units over M2 and 847.12
over M4, and licences them on the smaller. A second fixture, 193 \texttt{partial} pairs against 27
\texttt{identical} ones under a background that makes independent origin a poor account of both, clears M2 by
276.09 and misses M4 at 1.97, so it is licensed by nothing: that construction is the only reason the minimum
matters. M4 is therefore last, not decorative. Its attractor alpha vector predicts a concordance ordering over
the three dimension blocks --- functional-or-pressure-prone highest at \(q = \alpha/(\alpha + 1) = 0.714286\),
structural 0.5, arbitrary-or-expressive 0.285714 --- and the block profile of every pair is now pooled
and scored against it: the raw means pooled key-weightedly, the chance correction averaged over the
dimensions each block declares, both read against the anomaly scan's per-dimension baseline measured on
\emph{the same pair graph with the same statistic} and identified by SHA-256 in the published verdict. An
adjacency now has three outcomes, not two: a gap of at least the registered 0.05 in the predicted direction
holds, a gap of that size backwards reverses, and anything smaller is recorded as
\texttt{tie\_within\_the\_declared\_separability}, because this layer's own registration of that number says
``a reversal smaller than it is a tie, not a refutation,'' and the first version of the test scored a
\(0.0003\) gap as a refutation. A flat or reversed profile disqualifies M4 from being \emph{named} the
stage-1 leader: the ranking and every likelihood are published
unchanged and the verdict falls to \texttt{no\_decisive\_winner}. Where the two scales straddle that line ---
which is what this corpus produces, at \(-0.177\) raw against \(-0.017\) corrected --- the test issues
\texttt{block\_profile\_verdict\_depends\_on\_the\_similarity\_scale} and disqualifies nothing, because a rule
that strips a model's name on an arithmetic choice the analyst has not yet defended strips it on the
instrument. A pair set that reaches fewer than two of the
ranked blocks, or never reaches two of them together, reports \texttt{m4\_block\_profile\_not\_testable} and is
refused, since a test that is silent on thin data is a test that never runs; and the confirming outcome is
labelled the weakest confirmable one, because three blocks admit six orderings and a surviving prediction is
the absence of a refutation, not corroboration. The simulated floors did not move with this gate --- the power
generator's synthetic pairs carry no per-key block profile, and the recomputed
\texttt{results/model\_power\_simulation.json} is byte-identical to the published one --- and that is stated
rather than glossed over, so
a reader can check that the calibration still describes the rule in force. The severity is deliberate: a priced
thirteen-operator vocabulary will fit almost anything. Evidence counts are no longer asserted either. \texttt{--power} simulates each model as the
truth on the declared grid and derives the smallest pair count at which stage 1 names the generating model
decisively in 0.8 of replications \emph{for every one of the four} --- 220 pairs here, M1 the binding truth ---
and takes the 5th-percentile Kish effective count of that binding cell (29.051621) as a second floor, so
dependence inside a pair list is counted rather than assumed. The holdout folds by work, witness and lineage
\emph{cluster}, never by pair index, the margin must hold on the held-out clusters as well as on the whole
set, and the split publishes its own leakage checks rather than asking to be trusted. The earlier ``at least 20
dated route-typed pairs on a held-out half'' was an analyst's number with no derivation behind it; the tool
names it as superseded and refuses to fall back to it. The operator table's hash is recorded when it is
written, so a derivation's price cannot be edited after the result is known.

\textbf{Current status.} The layer runs and reports
\texttt{status: ``not\_yet\_testable''}, with two missing inputs: no candidate
derivation record, and no dated attestation --- 0 of 65 works carries a filled

\section{The limits of global reconstruction}

Comparative reconstruction is strongest where languages, textual histories and
historically documented relationships provide independent constraints. It becomes
weaker as comparisons move beyond those constraints. A shared name or formula
within a demonstrably related language family has a different evidentiary status
from a broad functional parallel between unrelated traditions. Likewise, a
uniquely shared sequence has more diagnostic value than a common theme, but only
if the alignment is not manufactured by flexible coding.

Three limits follow. First, the survival of evidence is uneven: literate and
archaeologically visible traditions are overrepresented, while many oral
traditions are known through relatively recent records. Second, narrative elements
do not behave as independent data points. Stories are copied, translated,
performed, revised and combined; a simple tree may therefore be less appropriate
than a network or hybrid model. Third, some historical processes are
observationally equivalent in surviving evidence: a shared feature may be
compatible with both inheritance and an undocumented contact event, and current
data may not distinguish them.

Independent invention must also be treated carefully. It is not proved merely
because researchers have failed to find a route of transmission. It becomes better
supported when the features in question recur under comparable conditions, lack
specific shared innovations, and have distributions incompatible with the proposed
historical route. Conversely, the absence of a known route does not make
convergence certain.

The global single-origin hypothesis is especially vulnerable to unfalsifiability if
every difference is classified as loss and every similarity as inheritance. A
defensible model must specify in advance which observations would favour multiple
families, transmission or convergence instead. If it can explain every outcome
equally well, it has no comparative predictive power.

Witzel's globally comparative reconstruction is the most developed published attempt at
the global genealogy this project was commissioned to hope for, and it is cited here for
that scope \citep{witzel2012}. Its large-scale groupings are historical hypotheses: under
the rules set out above, no such grouping could enter this paper's adjudication layer
without dated attestations, a route typed as descent or contact, and reviewed
counterevidence --- which is a statement about what this instrument may publish, not a
verdict on the reconstruction.

These limits do not make global comparison useless. They change its function.
Broad comparison can identify candidates, estimate motif distributions and reveal
recurring narrative solutions. Source-intensive regional investigation can then
test historical explanations. The strongest claims are likely to concern particular
families and episodes rather than an original narrative system for all humanity.

\section{Research implications}

A productive global project should proceed from evidence-rich regional studies
toward broader comparison, not from a universal genealogy toward confirming
examples. Computational retrieval can manage scale, but it cannot replace textual
criticism, historical linguistics or regional expertise. Graphs can display
disputed relationships, but the evidential status of every relationship must
remain visible.

Future work should therefore prioritise three outputs: a passage-linked corpus of
versions, independent annotations with reliability measures, and preregistered
comparisons with controls. Reconstruction attempts should be limited to families
whose relationships survive source criticism and alternative models. Negative
results and unresolved cases must remain in the published corpus to prevent a
file-drawer composed entirely of striking parallels.

For living traditions, scholarly access is not the only requirement. Community
authority, consent, restrictions and appropriate representation must shape
collection and publication. These obligations are also methodological: removing a
narrative from its performative and social context can destroy the evidence needed
to interpret it.

Four gates stand between the present instruments and any historical conclusion.
First, a second coder must independently annotate the same material: the
structural pilot's predicate families, motif definitions and comparison classes
were authored by the person who applied them, and the state coder's blinded
6{,}199-proposition packet exists, is scored by a tool that refuses one coder and
refuses a key whose probe rows carry no sampling weight, and
has never been opened. What has changed since this sentence was first written is
only the logistics: a 300-proposition pilot slice, its codebook and its
instructions are now generated files with published hashes, so the ask no longer
requires anything from the project except two readers who do not talk to each
other. Every D7 assignment, every affirmative-doctrine count and
every speech-act split in this paper is therefore single-analyst and
machine-classified. Second, adjudicated historical edges must be produced one at a
time by source criticism --- the adjudication layer of both exported graphs is
deliberately empty, and only a claim with dated witnesses, a stated route typed as
descent or contact, and reviewed counterevidence may enter it. Third, retrieval
must extend beyond English translation: the detector sets for Sanskrit, Pali,
Hebrew, Greek, Arabic, Akkadian and Chinese are the work that turns a
multilingual-ready frame into a multilingual one. Fourth, the corpus must be
balanced by independent narrative lineages rather than by work count; sixteen
South Asian works against one Polynesian collection is a sampling design, not a
distribution.

\section{Conclusion}

Narrative motifs demonstrably recur, but recurrence is not one historical
phenomenon. Common descent, borrowing, convergence and mixed development can all
produce resemblances, and different elements of a story may have different
histories. The source-critical pilot illustrates the descriptive differences
concealed by a broad category such as ``storm god and serpent'', and the
instrumented stages that follow show what happens when those categories are
operationalised rather than asserted.

Three instruments returned results that constrain the project's own hopes.
Lexically, 92.0\% of candidate pairs in a 65-work corpus join works already known
to depend on one another, so a similarity ranking built before source criticism
mostly ranks the history of printing and translation; excluding editorial
apparatus and undersized passages removes roughly two-thirds of the candidate set,
although the same validation that certifies the reduction shows seven annotated
evidence passages falling on excluded windows, so cleaning's narrative recall is
itself an open question. Structurally, an event-alignment engine that is invariant
to translator vocabulary and that separates its five components reproduces the
expected ordering for dependent witnesses, but declines to reproduce the expected
ordering for famous parallels: Noah--Gilgamesh scores 0.095 on events while
scoring 0.550 on adjudicated motifs, and the sequence measure is nearly blind to
event order for reasons of gap cost rather than reasons of myth. Conceptually, the
instrument built to test whether ``god'' names one thing worldwide first answered
its own question badly --- a universality test over English translation vocabulary,
reported as though it bore on ancestry --- and the corrected version answers
nothing at all yet, because its inheritance layer is empty by design, its
annotation packet has never been opened, and its model-comparison layer prints
\texttt{not\_yet\_testable}. Measured against a chance line drawn from its own
value frequencies, the same instrument reports that three of its twelve
dimensions fire on no work at all, that its speaker-attribution axis clears its
own base rate by \(+0.006\), and that the single prediction separating M4 from a
second null reverses on raw similarity and ties once each dimension's base rate
is subtracted --- which is why the falsification hook registered against M4 is
published here as a statement about the scale an analyst chooses to subtract, and
not as a verdict about these traditions.

The present evidence justifies continued investigation of particular narrative
families. It does not justify a claim that all mythologies descend from one
original system, and on current data it does not even license a distributional
claim, since every structural result rests on single-analyst coding of 14 versions
drawn from English translations. The scientifically valuable objective is
therefore not to complete a universal tree at any cost, but to establish which
relationships are supported, which are contradicted, and which are beyond
recovery. An unresolved comparison is a valid result; an invented ancestor is not.

The project was begun in the hope that these recurrences would show the concept of
a god to be one inherited fiction, and the honest report is what the instruments
did with that hope. Decomposed into its three propositions, only the first is
addressed by anything built here: that divine narratives share ancestry is a
question the lexical and structural instruments can at least fail to answer
cleanly; that the concept itself has one origin requires dated first attestations
and evidence that doctrinal vocabulary, not plot, travelled, which this apparatus
does not yet carry; and that no such being exists is not a claim any textual method
can reach, treating an idea's human origin as its refutation being a fallacy that
runs in both directions. Testing the precondition of the middle proposition ---
that ``god'' names one thing worldwide --- the first divinity instrument reported
ten concept grammars in the same 65 works with no grammar present in all of them,
and reported the doctrine of strict oneness as documented in 46 passages across
four works and four traditions. Neither report survives in the form it was given.
The distribution is true of translators' vocabulary and nothing more, and the
inference drawn from it --- that no stable concept exists for an inheritance
hypothesis to explain --- is withdrawn; the oneness count, audited match by match,
is 51 matches rather than 46 once the hard line-wraps are collapsed, its
\emph{Sacred Books of the East} hits sit inside a reprint of a Qur'an translation
this corpus already counts, two are substring collisions, three are prohibitions
whose grammar presupposes the gods they forbid, and what survives is 34 confessional
matches in two works not marked dependent under the configured rules, across 2
traditions --- a count that describes the instrument's own dependence list and does
not establish independence, because that list is not closed. An observation
that all four competing models predict equally cannot rank any of them, so this
corpus yields no negative verdict about common ancestry --- and, for the same
reason, no positive one. The hoped-for conclusion is not disproved; it is
untested. What the rebuild replaces it with is a machine capable of failing to
give an answer on purpose and of saying so: twelve analytically distinct dimensions read
proposition by proposition, three separate questions per record, nine values
declared undetectable, a candidate pair set enumerated exactly rather than sketched
and measured, instead of asserted as complete, a priced operator vocabulary with a path limit and a preregistration
hash, and a comparison layer whose current verdict is \texttt{not\_yet\_testable}
with the two missing inputs listed. What survives is the harder and more useful
programme: dated attestation, original-language evidence, independent coding, and
a claim-by-claim history of which divine ideas travelled, which converged, and
which we cannot tell.

\section*{Reproducibility statement}

\texttt{python -m pytest tests -q} runs the full suite offline. This file is checked
by \texttt{tools/validate\_tex.py}, which verifies environment nesting, every row's
cell count against its column specification, escape hygiene, and that each
cross-reference and citation key resolves. \texttt{python tools/validate\_tex.py
--self-test} then injects each defect class into an in-memory copy of this source and
requires the matching check to fire: all ten do, and the clean copy reports nothing.
It reports structure, not content. Compilation is a separate check, and here it is a
measurement rather than a disclaimer: \texttt{tests/test\_tex\_typesetting.py} copies
this source into an empty temporary directory and runs \texttt{pdflatex} there, repeating
the pass until the reported page
count repeats and the log stops asking for a rerun, and fails the suite if the engine
exits non-zero, if its log reports an overfull or underfull box, if a reference or
citation is still undefined on the final pass, if the length is still moving after four
passes, or if the page count the prose states is not the page count the engine produced.
On this machine (MiKTeX 25.12) the paper sets to 43 pages with none of those
occurring; the earlier edition of this paragraph said no \LaTeX{} engine was installed,
which was true of that machine and wrong as a reason not to look. Where no engine is
installed the test skips and says so, instead of leaving the geometry unverified in
silence. Each instrument
publishes the SHA-256 of the corpus slice, the configuration and the code that
produced it, and a test fails if any of those change after the run. The
reproducibility field of the state run records
\texttt{randomness: ``none: sorted iteration, no sampling, no fitted
parameters''}, and the graph's exact index has none either, since it reads its posting
lists in sorted order. Randomness survives only as seeded, published draws --- the
retrieval nulls and the structural engine's 200 permutations at seed 20261031, and the
blinded packet's samples at seed 20261101 --- plus the superseded MinHash step, whose
seed, band structure, bucket cap and measured recall are published in
Table~\ref{tab:graph} but which now produces no published edge. The gzipped artifacts
are written with \field{mtime} pinned to zero and no stored filename, because the
header's creation timestamp made two runs differ byte for byte while every count in
them stayed equal. Line endings are pinned by the same argument: every text-mode writer in \texttt{tools/} states the endings it emits, because an unstated policy would let a reviewer's operating system rewrite the artifacts and report every downstream stage as stale.

Internal freshness gates can only check this repository against itself, and the corpus has a
second reproducibility dependency that is external: its texts came from a publisher that edits
files in place. \texttt{tools/verify\_corpus\_live.py} re-downloads all 65 works and compares the
bytes it receives with the hash the manifest recorded, and
\texttt{results/corpus\_live\_verification.json} publishes the outcome per work. In the run
recorded here, 63 of 65 still match. Two do not. \texttt{australian\_legendary\_tales}
(Gutenberg 3833) is served at the same 207{,}276 bytes with two differing byte positions from
offset 184{,}973, where the acquired gloss ``E\"{a}r moonan'' reads ``Eaer moonan'' in the live
copy---one entry of a word list whose accented vowel is now transliterated, not a change of
narrative text. \texttt{odyssey} (Gutenberg 1727) is served two bytes shorter, its first
difference at offset 664 where the header \emph{Most recently updated: December 2, 2023} now
reads \emph{November 2, 2026}; its length changed, so the positional comparison reports
685{,}258 differing offsets, which is the arithmetic of a shift rather than 685{,}258 edits --
aligned line by line the two copies differ at 4 sites: that header date, a numeral restored
in the preface, a footnote marker added to a body line, and one separator line holding only a slash removed near the end. The point of publishing
either is not that a drift is large but that a fresh
acquisition on this machine no longer reproduces every shipped file. A reviewer who re-runs the
fetcher should therefore expect these two hashes to differ, and the manifest with the hashes it
recorded---not whatever the live site serves today---is the authority for every measurement in
this paper.
